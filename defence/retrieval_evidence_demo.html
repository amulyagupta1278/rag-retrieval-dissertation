When a citizen asks about a welfare scheme, which retriever finds the right rule?

Twenty-two Indian government policy documents. Thirty-four questions written and graded by hand. Five retrieval systems given exactly the same corpus, the same chunks, and the same relevance judgements. This page shows what each system actually returned — not a summary of it.

01

Why this matters

A retrieval error in a policy assistant is not an academic inconvenience. If the system surfaces the wrong scheme document, the answer that reaches the citizen is confidently wrong: an ineligible applicant is told they qualify, or a qualified applicant is told they do not. The generator cannot repair evidence it never received. That makes retrieval — not generation — the component that determines whether the answer is safe.

Headline result — contradicts the prevailing assumption

On this corpus, BM25 — a keyword ranking function from 1994 — beat modern dense embeddings on every question category except one. The gap was widest on paraphrase queries, the exact case dense retrieval is claimed to solve.

04

What the evidence supports

01

Domain vocabulary beats semantic similarity

Policy text is a bounded, highly standardised vocabulary. Scheme names, acronyms and statutory phrases repeat verbatim across documents. Lexical matching exploits that structure directly; a general-purpose embedding trained on web text dilutes it.

02

The paraphrase result is the surprising one

H2 predicted dense retrieval would win on paraphrased questions. It lost by half. The rewrites preserved rare content words — "cooking", "income", "housing" — which lexical matching still anchors on, while the embedding spread probability across thematically similar but incorrect schemes.

03

Fusion and reranking recover the losses

Hybrid RRF and LLM reranking both close the gap and lead on aggregate scores. But reranking carries a latency and per-query cost that a citizen-facing service at volume has to justify against a measurable quality gain.

04

The graph baseline failed, and the reason is informative

Entity-co-occurrence retrieval scored zero on every paraphrase question. Without the literal scheme name in the query, the entity linker had nothing to anchor to — a concrete limit of NER-dependent retrieval on this domain, not a tuning error.
